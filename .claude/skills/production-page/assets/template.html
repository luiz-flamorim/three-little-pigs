The story

Step one: the story. It is improvised like a tabletop role-playing game. A Storyteller sets the scene and quietly runs the rounds, while the three pigs and the Wolf each play their own part, one short turn at a time, always going along with whatever the others bring in. After every turn a real die is rolled. On an unlucky roll, chaos strikes: something from another tale bursts in, and the story can swerve somewhere nobody planned. It always ends on a cliffhanger, ready for the next chapter.

{{STORY}}

The screenplay

Step two: the screenplay. A story written to be read is not yet ready to be seen. A screenwriter reads it like a film director planning shots: it breaks the action into panels, adds the moments in between and the close-ups that make it clear, adds big sound words, and trims the text to the bare minimum. It saves the most dramatic shot for the very end.

{{SCREENPLAY}}

The panels

Step three: the panels. Each panel is drawn in code, in one fixed style: a single dark ink, thick outlines and thin details, shapes built from circles, arcs and straight lines, with real perspective. The characters are reusable puppets, so they look the same in every panel. Here is every panel on its own.

{{PANELS}}

The comic

Step four: the comic. The panels are laid out on one widescreen page, in rectangles, squares and slanted shapes, arranged so the page reads naturally from left to right and top to bottom. The words are lettered in, and the whole page is saved as a single file you can open anywhere.
